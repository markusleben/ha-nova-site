Three steps to connect

No terminal skills needed. On HA OS or Supervised, you paste one command and a wizard guides you. Then you sign in with Home Assistant once.

  1. Run the installer. Paste one command on macOS, Linux, or Windows. The wizard finds your Home Assistant.
  2. Sign in with Home Assistant. Your browser opens the Home Assistant login. Sign in as the owner. The wizard sets up the NOVA Relay App if needed, and this computer connects by itself.
  3. Get a first look. Setup sums up your home in one line. It points out up to two things to check, like a low battery. It also gives you example requests with your own room and device names.

What you can do

Talk to the AI app you already use. HA NOVA does the careful part.

You say What happens

“Check my automations for problems”

Checks them against 40+ rules. Finds conflicts, dead triggers, and mistakes that are hard to spot.

“Why didn’t my motion light trigger last night?”

Replays what really happened. Shows where it failed and why.

“Turn on the porch light at sunset and off at 11 PM”

Builds the automation and checks it. Shows it to you before it saves anything.

“What happened to my living room temperature overnight?”

Reads the history and tells you what happened, in plain words.

“Build a dashboard for the living room”

Builds the dashboard with the custom cards you have. Lists every view and card that changes. Saves a copy first, so “undo that” brings it back.

“Turn off the kitchen light”

Turns it off right away. Then checks that it really is off. Whole rooms and scenes show a preview first.

“Unlock the front door”

Shows what will happen, then waits for you to confirm. You click Confirm in a window, tap your linked phone, or type a one-time code.

“What do I have, and where is this sensor used?”

Counts your rooms, devices, automations, and integrations. Lists the automations, scripts, scenes, groups, helpers, and dashboard cards that use the sensor. Says what it couldn’t check. Exact numbers, never a guess.

“My door sensor died, I paired a new one”

Moves automations, scripts, scenes, dashboards, helpers, and energy sources to the new device. You check it all as one plan.

“Turn this Node-RED flow into an automation”

Explains what the flow does and finds each device. Builds a normal Home Assistant automation. Turns the old rule off, but keeps it.

“Check my ESPHome config for the garage sensor”

Explains the device file in plain words. Flags removed settings, pins that can’t do the job, and passwords written into the file.

Safe by design

Safety is the core of HA NOVA. Here is what happens when the AI wants to change something.

  1. Looks first

    Reads your real devices, automations, and past runs first. No guessing.

  2. Shows the exact change

    You see exactly what will change before anything is saved. Only one clear everyday action, like turning off one light, skips this.

  3. You approve

    Everything else waits for your yes. HA NOVA decides what needs it, never the AI.

    Locks, gates, the alarm, restarting Home Assistant, and deletes need more than a yes. You confirm them on your computer or your phone:

    • Click Confirm in a window on your computer
    • Tap your linked phone

    No screen and no phone? You type a one-time code. The AI can see it too, so it only counts when you type it yourself.

    More about confirming

    Window. On a computer with a screen, a window opens. Click Confirm (OK on Windows) or Cancel. Your AI never sees a code.

    Phone. No screen, like on a Linux server? Then the one phone you linked asks, in the Home Assistant Companion app. (Not with a standalone relay that runs on your own token.) You can answer on the locked phone or its Apple Watch. So whoever holds your phone can answer too, but only for that one action, and only while it waits (up to 90 seconds). To link, move, or turn off phone confirmation, you must unlock the phone. Once a phone is linked, an action it can’t reach is refused, never moved to chat.

    Code. With neither, you get a four-digit one-time code, in chat or in your AI app’s own form where it shows one.

    Automation, script, and dashboard deletes use the window or the phone. Other deletes, like helpers, scenes, and areas, use a four-digit code in chat.

    If anything changed since the preview, nothing is sent.

    Careful: anything you let control your screen can click that window too. That means computer use, apps you gave Accessibility access, and on Windows and some Linux desktops, other programs. To turn the window off, run ha-nova setup confirm-window off.

    Every safety rule, and the test behind it: the safety page on GitHub.

  4. Saves a way back

    Before a covered delete or a full replacement, HA NOVA saves a copy of the item.

  5. Saves and checks

    Saves the change, then reads it back to check that it matches what you approved.

  6. Checks its own work

    Looks for mistakes, conflicts, and things that may break later.

  7. Offers a safe test

    After saving an automation or script, you get a test plan that fits the risk. Before a real run, it names the devices that will switch and waits for your go.

  8. Lets you undo

    Reply revert to undo the latest checked update. Deleted items come back from their saved copy.

    More about undo

    Saved copies cover deleted automations, scripts, scenes, dashboards, and storage helpers. “Undo that” right after a delete works too.

    Revert and saved copies only cover changes made through HA NOVA. For anything else, use a Home Assistant Backup. It covers the whole system.

Which changes get all eight steps

Automations and scripts get all eight steps. Helpers get a preview and a check. Dashboards show every changed view and card, save a copy first, and can be undone. For devices, one clear everyday action runs right away and gets checked. Everything else shows a preview first.

Ground rules

  • The Relay’s Home Assistant login never reaches the AI.

  • On App installs, each device gets its own connection. Sign-in works once and expires in minutes. One click in NOVA cuts a device off. (The standalone container uses one shared token.)

  • Every rule the AI follows is a plain text file you can read.

  • No HA NOVA cloud in the middle. No usage tracking. No install counting.

At home and away Beta

At home, your computer talks to Home Assistant directly. “Local only” stays the default. Away, it can use your paid Home Assistant Link plan (formerly Home Assistant Cloud). It switches by itself. Your login stays in your computer’s own safe storage.

You need HA OS or Supervised, with remote access turned on. You also need a desktop session to approve it once. Container, Core, WSL, headless, service, and gateway setups work at home only.

How it works

The NOVA Relay is an App on your Home Assistant server. When you connect locally, it is the only part that talks to Home Assistant. Everything the AI knows lives in skills: plain text files on your computer. The relay stays dumb. The skills stay smart.

Some checks must never depend on the AI. So the HA NOVA program on your computer runs them. It decides what needs a preview or your confirmation. It stops a change if anything changed since the preview.

Skills first, not a tool catalog

A typical tool server loads its whole tool list into your AI by default. HA NOVA hands it one skill when needed: a text file you can open and read.

Get started

You need Home Assistant. Any install type works. On HA OS and Supervised, the wizard sets up the NOVA Relay App. Container and Core run the same relay as a standalone container, set up with a token.

macOS / Linux

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markusleben/ha-nova/main/install.sh | bash

Windows (PowerShell)

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markusleben/ha-nova/main/install.ps1 | iex

No browser, using SSH, or not the Home Assistant owner? Then you connect with a six‑digit code from the NOVA page instead.

Connect with a code

The wizard opens the NOVA Relay App page: click “Add”, then Install and Start. (Skip this if the App already runs.) The owner opens NOVA in the Home Assistant sidebar and clicks “Connect a device”. You type the six-digit code. It works once and expires in minutes.

Over SSH, this only works well on Linux. On Windows and macOS, run setup on the computer itself. Over SSH, HA NOVA usually can’t save its login there.

Want to look first? The demo house is a test Home Assistant with no real devices. Everyone shares one login, and it resets every hour. So never enter real names, passwords, or tokens there.
