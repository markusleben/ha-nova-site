Three steps to connect

No terminal expertise needed. On HA OS or Supervised, the installer and the wizard do the wiring; a one-time sign-in with Home Assistant does the trust.

  1. Run the installer. One command for macOS, Linux, or Windows. The wizard finds your Home Assistant.
  2. Sign in with Home Assistant. The wizard opens your Home Assistant login in the browser. Sign in as the owner: the wizard installs and starts the NOVA Relay App for you if needed, and this computer connects by itself.
  3. Get a first look. Setup ends with your home in one line, up to two things worth a look — a low battery, an automation that can’t run — and example prompts with your own room and device names.

No browser here, or signed in as another Home Assistant user? The wizard shows the NOVA Relay App page instead (click “Add”, then Install and Start; skipped when the App already runs), and you pair with a one-time code: the Home Assistant owner clicks “Connect a device” on the NOVA page in the sidebar, and you type the six‑digit code. Over SSH this works reliably on Linux only: on Windows and macOS, run setup in a terminal on that computer’s own desktop, because over SSH HA NOVA usually can’t save its credential. Sign-in and the code are one-time and expire in minutes; each device gets its own connection you can revoke anytime from NOVA.

What you can do

You talk to the assistant you already use. HA NOVA does the careful part.

You say What happens

“Check my automations for problems”

Audits against 40+ rules — catches conflicts, dead triggers, and mistakes you’d never spot manually.

“Why didn’t my motion light trigger last night?”

Replays the actual trace and shows exactly where it failed and why.

“Turn on the porch light at sunset and off at 11 PM”

Builds, previews, and reviews the automation before it touches your config.

“What happened to my living room temperature overnight?”

Pulls history and summarizes the timeline in plain language.

“Build a dashboard for the living room”

Builds whole dashboards and views with the custom cards you installed, lists every view and card that changes, and saves a copy first — “undo that” restores it.

“Turn off the kitchen light”

Turns it off right away and checks that it really is off.

“Unlock the front door”

Shows exactly what will happen, then waits for you: Confirm in a window on your computer, a tap on your linked phone, or a one-time code.

“What do I have, and where is this sensor used?”

Counts rooms, devices, automations, and integrations itself and lists the automations, scripts, scenes, groups, helpers, and dashboard cards that use a sensor, and says what it couldn’t check — exact numbers, never a guess.

“My door sensor died, I paired a new one”

Moves automations, scripts, scenes, dashboards, helpers, and energy sources to the new device in one reviewed plan.

“Turn this Node-RED flow into an automation”

Lists what the rule does, maps each device to a real entity, builds a native automation, and keeps the old rule disabled, not deleted.

“Check my ESPHome config for the garage sensor”

Explains the device file in plain words and flags removed keys, pins that can’t do what the file asks, and passwords written into it.

Safe by design

Safety is the product, not a setting. Automations and scripts run the full workflow below; helpers are previewed and verified; device control runs unambiguous everyday actions right away and verifies them, everything else is previewed first; dashboards show every changed view and card, save a copy first, and can be undone.

  1. Researches first

    Reads your real entities, automations, and traces before proposing anything. No guessing.

  2. Shows the exact change

    For anything beyond an unambiguous everyday action, you see the precise diff before a single byte is written.

  3. You approve

    Unambiguous everyday actions run right away and are checked afterwards; everything else waits for your yes. Unlocking a door, opening a gate or garage, disarming the alarm, restarting Home Assistant, and deletes need your explicit confirmation — HA NOVA decides which is which, never the AI. If anything changed since the preview, nothing is sent.

    Where your computer can show one, a confirmation window opens: click Confirm (OK on Windows) or Cancel, and your AI never sees a code. Without a desktop (for example a headless Linux machine), the one phone you linked asks (Home Assistant Companion app; not with a standalone relay that runs on your own token). You answer on the phone or its Apple Watch without unlocking it, so whoever holds your phone can answer while a request is open (up to 90 seconds), and only for that one action; linking, moving, or turning off phone confirmation needs the unlocked phone. Once a phone is linked, an action it can’t reach is refused, never moved to chat. With neither, you get a four-digit one-time code, in chat or in your AI app’s own form where it shows one; the AI can see it too, so it only counts when you type it yourself. Automation, script, and dashboard deletes use the same window or phone; other deletes (helpers, scenes, areas, and the like) take a four-digit code in chat.

    Anything you allow to control your screen — computer use, or apps you granted Accessibility; on Windows and some Linux desktops, other programs — can click that window too. Turn it off with ha-nova setup confirm-window off.

  4. Captures a recovery point

    Before a covered delete or full replacement, HA NOVA snapshots the item it is about to change, so “gone” has a way back.

  5. Writes and verifies

    Applies the change, then checks that what landed is what you approved.

  6. Audits itself

    Reviews its own work afterwards: mistakes, conflicts, reliability issues.

  7. Offers a safe test

    After saving an automation or script, you get a test plan matched to the risk — from a zero-impact logic check to a real run that names which devices will switch before you say go.

  8. Offers a way back

    Reply revert to undo the latest verified update. Deleted automations, scripts, scenes, dashboards, and storage helpers can be restored from their config snapshot — “undo that” right after a delete works too; beyond that, a Home Assistant Backup. Covers changes made through HA NOVA.

Ground rules

  • Relay credentials stay out of the AI.

  • Every device is paired on its own: Home Assistant sign-in or a six-digit code, both one-time and short-lived, revocable with one click from NOVA on App installs.

  • Every rule the AI follows is a markdown file you can read.

  • No HA NOVA-operated cloud relay, no usage analytics, no installation counting.

At home and away Beta

Local access stays first; your own Home Assistant Link subscription (formerly Home Assistant Cloud) becomes the automatic fallback — no manual URL switching, and credentials stay in your OS’s native credential store. Requires HA OS or Supervised with remote access enabled and a desktop session for the one-time authorization; Container, Core, WSL, and headless setups stay local-only.

How it works

A lean NOVA Relay runs as an App on your Home Assistant server and, on the local path, is the only component that talks to Home Assistant. Everything the AI knows lives in readable markdown skills on your machine. The relay stays dumb; the skills stay smart. The checks that must never depend on the AI — which actions need a preview or your confirmation, whether anything changed since the preview — run in the HA NOVA command on your machine.

Skills first, not a tool catalog

A typical tool server pushes its whole catalog into your assistant by default. HA NOVA hands it one skill, on demand: a markdown file you can open and read.

Get started

You need Home Assistant — any install type. HA OS and Supervised get the NOVA Relay App via the wizard; Container and Core run the same relay as a standalone container with a token-based setup.

macOS / Linux

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markusleben/ha-nova/main/install.sh | bash

Windows (PowerShell)

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/markusleben/ha-nova/main/install.ps1 | iex

Want to look first? The demo house is a simulated Home Assistant with no real devices, to look around before you connect your own. Everyone shares one login and it resets every hour, so never enter real names, passwords, or tokens there.
